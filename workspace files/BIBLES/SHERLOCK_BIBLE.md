# Scene 1: A Plea to Baker Street
**Setting:** 221B Baker Street, London. Late 19th Century. The room is thick with the scent of chemical reagents and stale tobacco.
**Characters:** Sherlock Holmes (Analytical), Dr. John Watson (Grounded), Romeo Montague (Urgent).
**Dramatic Goal:** Romeo secures Holmes’s assistance to locate the abducted Juliet.
**Atmospheric Tone:** Claustrophobic, intellectual vs. emotional, urgent.

#### Raw Prose (Master)
The London fog pressed against the glass of 221B Baker Street like a living, suffocating shroud. Inside, the laboratory was a sanctuary of order. Sherlock Holmes stood hunched over a rack of test tubes, his silhouette sharp and angular against the gaslight. The air was heavy with the sharp, acidic tang of iodine and the low, rhythmic hum of a Bunsen burner. 

Dr. John Watson sat by the hearth, nursing a lukewarm tea and observing his friend’s focused industry. The silence was absolute until it was shattered by a frantic, rhythmic pounding against the street-level door. It was not the knock of a caller, but the desperate battering of a soul at the end of its tether.

Before Watson could rise, Holmes had already abandoned his work, wiping his hands on a stained silk cloth. The door swung open to reveal a young man whose condition spoke of miles traveled in terror. Romeo Montague collapsed across the threshold, his coat singed and matted with the grime of the city, his fingers white-knuckled around a charred, leather-bound journal.

“I was told,” Romeo gasped, his voice trembling with a frantic, poetic cadence, “only a man who deals in impossible truths could help me. My Juliet… she is gone.”

Holmes did not offer a hand to help the young man rise. Instead, he stood over him, eyes scanning the youth with the clinical detachment of a surgeon examining a tumor. He moved with a calculated grace, his presence filling the room with a sudden, icy intellect.

“Your pulse is erratic,” Holmes observed, his voice clipping the air with precision. “Your clothes smell of sulfur—a byproduct of the industrial district’s lower docks—and you are clearly desperate. You have run until your lungs burned, and you have carried that journal as if it were the Holy Grail. Speak. But be brief.”

Romeo scrambled to his feet, eyes wild. “The Capulets. They took her. Her father’s spies—they did not just take her; they erased her. I have nothing left but this, and the truth of what they are hiding.”

Watson stepped forward, his hand steadying Romeo’s shaking shoulder. “Easy, lad. Breathe.”

Holmes turned to his mantle, reaching for a thick, dusty dossier tucked behind a row of chemistry texts. He opened it, revealing clippings and ink-blotted maps of a sprawling criminal syndicate in Verona. The ink on the files was old, but the names were freshly underscored in red. 

“The Capulet family,” Holmes murmured, his tone shifting from clinical to predatory. “I have been cataloging their rise from noble house to criminal empire for seven years. I had hoped they would overreach, and it seems, in their obsession with this girl, they finally have. You seek a rescue, Mr. Montague, but you have stumbled into a hornet's nest. Tell me exactly where you last saw them, and do not spare the details, however painful.”

Romeo looked at the file, then back at the detective. For the first time, the terror in his eyes was eclipsed by a glimmer of hope. He began to speak, his words tumbling out in a rush, painting a portrait of a city held under the iron grip of a shadow.
